# 45 — A boarder paying $245 a week or less is not taxable income

**Audience:** homeowners with boarders or home-stay students. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** January and February, when home-stay students arrive.
Works any time. Keep it a few weeks clear of posts 23 and 43.
**Source guide:** none on the site yet.
**Why it works:** a single weekly number that settles the question for most
households, and a hard limit on how many boarders it covers.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the weekly standard cost **and the income year it belongs to**. Last
  known: **$245 a week per boarder for the 2025-26 income year** ($237 for
  2024-25) —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rules-for-working-out-rental-income-and-expenses/standard-cost-method-for-boarders-and-home-stay-students
  It is CPI-adjusted each year and published around June for the year just
  ended (DET 19/01 adjustments on taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz). **Check for a
  newer figure and name the income year on the slide.**
- VERIFY the limit: "up to a maximum of 4" boarders or home-stay students, "at
  all times throughout the income year"; with five or more the standard-cost
  method cannot be used — same page.
- VERIFY: where income is within the standard cost you do not need to file a
  return for that income, keep expense records, or pay tax on it; above it, the
  excess is taxable and a return is required — same page and worksheet IR1255.
- VERIFY: "You cannot claim any losses from providing a boarding or home-stay
  student service."
- **Important nuance.** The full standard-cost calculation in IR1255 also
  includes annual housing and transport amounts, so the true tax-free level can
  be higher than $245 a week. The post says $245 or less is safe, which is
  conservative and correct. **Do not say that anything over $245 is taxable.**
  Slide 4 points to the worksheet instead.
- **DO NOT STATE** what distinguishes a boarder from a flatmate unless IRD's
  wording is found on the day.

## Caption
Hook: `If a boarder or home-stay student pays you $245 a week or less, that money is not taxable income.`

Then: Inland Revenue sets a standard weekly cost for having a boarder or
home-stay student in your own home. For the 2025-26 income year it is $245 per
boarder. Where what you are paid is within the standard cost, there is no tax
on it, no return to file for it and no receipts to keep. Above that, the
calculation gets fuller, because the method also allows for housing and
transport costs, so the right step is Inland Revenue's worksheet, not an
assumption. Two limits apply. The method covers up to four boarders at any one
time, and it cannot produce a loss to claim elsewhere. The figure changes every
year, so check which year you are looking at.
