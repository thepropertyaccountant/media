# 55 — Bright-line tax is on the profit. Know what comes off the price.

**Audience:** residential rental owners and investors. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Leave a few weeks between this and the other
bright-line posts (05, 09, 35, 40, 49, 53).
**Source guide:** guides/bright-line-test.html
**Why it works:** sellers who know they are caught then overpay, because they
subtract only the purchase price. The renovation invoices they could never
deduct finally matter.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the list against the bright-line guide IR1229 (March 2026 edition,
  working out the profit) —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir1200---ir1299/ir1229/bright-line-property-tax.pdf
  Last known: "The cost of the property includes: the amount you paid to buy
  the property (the initial purchase price of the property); expenditure
  related to the purchase, for example lawyers, valuers, surveyors and real
  estate agents fees; any capital improvements made to the property after
  buying it, for example renovations; expenditure related to the disposal, for
  example lawyers and real estate agent fees." And: "you cannot include
  anything you have already claimed a deduction for".
- VERIFY slide 6: "Personal expenses are not deductible." and that where the
  property was vacant and not earning income, holding costs are not deductible
  — IR1229, same section.
- **DO NOT STATE** a rule for interest, rates or insurance on a property that
  was rented. IR1229's treatment of holding costs has several limbs (it also
  says "Any interest expenditure prior to the date of the agreement to sell the
  property is not deductible" in one context). Slide 5 says only that what was
  already claimed is not claimed again. Read pp. 42–44 in full before adding
  anything on holding costs.
- **DO NOT STATE** the rate of tax on the gain. Inland Revenue's guides say
  the profit is included in the return; they do not say "marginal rate" in
  words.

## Caption
Hook: `If the bright-line test catches your sale, the tax is on the profit, and the profit is smaller than sale price minus purchase price.`

Then: the cost of the property for this purpose is more than what you paid. It
includes the costs of buying, such as legal, valuation and survey fees. It
includes capital improvements made after you bought, which is the renovation
you were never allowed to deduct against the rent. And it includes the costs of
selling, such as the agent's commission and the legal fees. What you cannot do
is count anything twice: a cost already claimed as a deduction does not come
off again. Personal costs do not come off at all. For anyone selling inside the
two years, the practical job is to gather the purchase file and every invoice
for capital work before the return is prepared, because each one of them
reduces the taxable figure.
