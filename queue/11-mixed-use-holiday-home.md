# 11 — The bach that sat empty: mixed-use asset rules

**Audience:** residential rental / holiday home owners. **Suggested:** 2026-12-04
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why now:** December. Baches go on Airbnb for the summer and the owner assumes
they can claim the year's rates and insurance. The 62-day test decides that, and
"available to rent" does not help them.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY: the mixed-use asset test — private use, income-earning use, AND
  **unused for 62 days or more** in the income year —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/mixed-use-assets
- VERIFY the trap on slide 3, which is the whole point of the post: on the
  holiday home page the words are that **being available for use does not
  count** as use. Confirm this wording before building, and use IRD's own word
  "unused", not "vacant" —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/tax-by-rental-property-type/renting-out-a-holiday-home
- VERIFY: the apportionment formula — income-earning days divided by
  (income-earning days + private days), applied to the expenses that need
  apportioning. Note that unused days are **not** in the denominator; confirm
  this reading on the work-out-expenses sub-page before stating it.
- VERIFY: income-earning use means a non-associated person paying **80% or more
  of market rates**; anything below that, or any use by you or an associated
  person, is private use.
- VERIFY: the opt-out — gross income from income-earning use **under $4,000**,
  or the asset is loss-making and that income is **under 2% of its value**. Also
  confirm the carve-out that these exemptions **do not apply to holiday homes
  owned by companies**.
- VERIFY: the quarantine rule — where income is under 2% of the home's value and
  expenses exceed income, deductions are capped at the income and the excess
  carries forward.
- VERIFY: how "value" is measured for the 2% test (the more recent of rateable
  value and purchase price, for property).
- Do NOT bring in the $50,000 boat and aircraft threshold. The audience owns
  houses, and it clutters the post.

## Caption
Hook: `Your bach earned well over summer. It is the months nobody was in it that decide the tax.`

Then: if a holiday home is used privately, used to earn income, and sits unused
for 62 days or more in the income year, the mixed-use asset rules apply to the
whole year. The trap is in that word unused — having it listed and available
does not count. A night nobody booked is an unused night no matter how long the
listing was up, which is how an owner who felt busy all summer ends up over the
threshold. From there you apportion expenses by the nights actually used, split
between earning and private, and the empty nights buy you nothing. There are two
releases: income under $4,000 lets you leave the whole thing out of your return,
though not if the home is owned by a company; and where the home runs at a loss
on income under 2% of its value, the deduction is capped at that income and the
rest carries forward. Worth counting the nights before you plan around a
deduction.
