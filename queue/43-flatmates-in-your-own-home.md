# 43 — Flatmate in your own home? The rent is income.

**Audience:** homeowners letting a room; rental owners living in part of a
property. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** January to March, when flats re-form for the year. Works
any time. Keep it a few weeks clear of post 45 (boarders).
**Source guide:** none on the site yet.
**Why it works:** a formula with Inland Revenue's own worked numbers. People
either ignore the income entirely or claim half the house.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY: an owner renting a room to a flatmate "may have tax to pay on the
  profit from renting to them" and uses the actual cost method; a tenant who
  just collects rent to pass on to the landlord has no taxable income from it —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/tax-by-rental-property-type/renting-out-a-room-in-my-main-home
- VERIFY the apportionment and the worked example —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rules-for-working-out-rental-income-and-expenses/actual-cost-method/working-out-expenses-when-you-live-in-the-property
  Last known: exclusive-use area divided by total floor area; "You can claim
  half of the expenses calculated using this percentage for shared common
  areas"; the two percentages added; then multiplied by income-earning days
  over days in the year. IRD's example: a 25 m² room in a 100 m² house is 25%;
  30 m² of common area is 30% halved to 15%; total 40%; then by days rented
  (for example 300 of 365).
- VERIFY: "you can only claim expenses for the time the room is rented."
- VERIFY which expenses the percentage applies to before naming any on
  slide 7. If the page gives a list, use its list. If it does not, keep slide 7
  to "the household costs you already pay".
- The short-stay and boarder standard-cost methods are different rules (posts
  23 and 45). Do not mix them into this post.

## Caption
Hook: `If you own your home and a flatmate pays you rent, that rent is income. The deduction against it is a formula.`

Then: tenants who split the rent and pass it to the landlord have nothing to
declare. An owner with a flatmate does. The profit is taxable, and the costs
are apportioned by floor area and by time. The flatmate's own room counts in
full, the shared areas count at half, and the result applies only for the days
the room was actually rented. Inland Revenue's example is a 25 square metre
room in a 100 square metre house with 30 square metres shared: 25% plus 15%,
so 40% of the costs, then scaled for a room rented 300 days of the year. It
takes a tape measure and ten minutes, and it replaces both common mistakes,
which are declaring nothing and claiming half the house.
