# 65 — The drive to your rental is deductible. With a record.

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. December to February suits it, when owners visit
between tenancies.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** almost every self-managing landlord drives to the property
and almost none log it. Small money, but real, and it comes with a rate.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY: the rental expenses page lists "travel expenses for travelling to
  inspect your property or to do repairs" as deductible —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rental-expense-deductions
- VERIFY, IR264 (March 2026 edition, motor vehicle expenses): "Use the Inland
  Revenue kilometre rates." "To claim kilometre rates you need to keep a
  vehicle logbook and record the date, distance travelled and reason for each
  trip related to your rental activity." "If you do not maintain actual records
  or a logbook, then the default method applies to limit your motor vehicle
  expenses." —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
- VERIFY slide 6, IR264: "If you travel using other transport and the trip is
  solely to inspect or do maintenance on the rental property the cost is
  usually fully deductible."
- VERIFY the kilometre rate used on slide 5 **and the income year it belongs
  to**. Last known Tier 1 rate for a petrol vehicle, **2025-26 income year:
  $1.20** per kilometre. Rates are published around June for the year just
  ended; check for a newer set —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses
  If the rate has moved, redo the last row of slide 5.
- **DO NOT STATE** what the default method allows without a logbook. IR264
  says it limits the claim; the detail was a summary, not a quote.
- **DO NOT STATE** that trips to look at properties you might buy are
  deductible. Not covered, and likely capital.
- Slide 5 is an illustration. Label it and check the arithmetic:
  14 × 38 = 532; 532 × 1.20 = 638.40, shown as $638.

## Caption
Hook: `Every trip you make to inspect or repair your rental is deductible. Very few landlords can show how many there were.`

Then: travel to inspect the property or to do repairs is a deductible rental
expense, and the simple way to claim it is Inland Revenue's kilometre rates: a
set amount per kilometre in place of adding up fuel, servicing and insurance.
The condition is a record. A logbook showing the date, the distance and the
reason for each trip. Without one, the claim is limited. A fortnightly drive
across town adds up to several hundred dollars a year, which is not life
changing and is also yours. A ferry or a flight made solely for the rental is
usually fully deductible on the same reasoning. The whole thing costs a note in
your phone each time you go.
