# 09 — The intention test outlives the bright-line period

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Suggested:** 2027-01-25
**Source guide:** guides/bright-line-test.html
**Why now:** two years after the period shortened to two years, sellers are
clearing the bright-line window and assuming that is the end of it.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY: bright-line period, last known **2 years** for sales on or after
  1 July 2024 — https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/buying-and-selling/bright-line
- VERIFY: the intention test — property bought with a purpose or intention of
  resale is taxable on sale regardless of how long it was held, and this sits
  outside the bright-line rules —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/buying-and-selling/when-you-need-to-pay
- VERIFY: the other land rules that can apply independently of bright-line
  (dealer, developer, builder associations; major development or division).
  Name only the ones confirmed, and describe them in plain words.
- VERIFY: there is no time limit on the intention test before asserting it.

## Caption
Hook: `You cleared the two-year bright-line window. That does not make the sale tax free.`

Then: bright-line is a date test — a bright line, which is the point of the
name. Clearing it only means that particular rule does not catch you. The
intention test sits underneath it: if the property was bought with a purpose or
intention of resale, the gain is taxable whenever you sell, with no time limit
running in your favour. What decides it is evidence created at the time you
bought — what you told the bank, how you financed it, what you did with it, and
whether this is a pattern. That evidence cannot be built later, which is the
whole reason to think about it early.
