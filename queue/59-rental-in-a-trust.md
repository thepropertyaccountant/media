# 59 — Rental in a trust? $10,001 of profit is taxed at 39%.

**Audience:** residential rental owners who hold property in a family trust.
**Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** January to March, before the trust's year closes. Works any
time. Keep it a few weeks clear of post 60.
**Source guide:** none on the site yet.
**Why it works:** a rate, a threshold and a cliff. Plenty of Auckland rentals
sit in trusts set up years ago, on returns nobody has looked at since the rate
changed.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY every sentence —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/trusts-and-estates/income/trustee-tax-rates
  Last known: "The information below applies to trusts and estates from 1 April
  2024 for income that is not distributed to beneficiaries. For earlier years,
  this income was taxed at a flat 33% rate." "If your trust earns $10,000 or
  less in a tax year (after annual deductions and before losses brought
  forward), you'll pay at a 33% rate." "If your trust earns more than $10,000,
  you'll need to pay 39%."
- **CONFIRMED 14 November 2026 — the cliff, which is the hook.** It is a
  de minimis threshold, not a marginal band. Inland Revenue's Special Report
  on the 39% trustee tax rate (9 April 2024) says: "Trusts with more than
  $10,000 trustee income (after deductible expenses) are subject to the 39%
  trustee tax rate on all of their trustee income." Its flowchart labels the
  two outcomes "39% tax rate on all trustee income" and "33% tax rate on all
  trustee income" —
  https://www.taxpolicy.ird.govt.nz/publications/2024/2024-sr-39-trustee-tax-rate
  So slide 1's "All of it. Not just the dollar over the line." is supported.
  Recheck on the day; if the Special Report cannot be opened, fall back to
  "Rental in a trust? Over $10,000 of profit is taxed at 39%" and cut slide 4.
- **Measurement caveats that go with the cliff — do not overstate it.** The
  $10,000 is *net trustee income*: after deductible expenses, before losses
  brought forward, and excluding income distributed or allocated to
  beneficiaries. The test is per trust, per income year. The statutory hook is
  section HC 40 of the Income Tax Act 2007, which has not been read directly.
- Do not say or imply that trusts can be split to stay under the threshold.
  The Special Report warns that fragmenting a trust to use the 33% rate
  "would raise tax avoidance concerns for the Commissioner".
- VERIFY the exceptions before implying the rate is universal: deceased estates
  for a limited period, disabled beneficiary trusts and energy consumer trusts
  stay at 33%. The caption says "most family trusts".
- VERIFY slide 5: the rate applies to income not distributed to beneficiaries,
  and distributions to minors are taxed at 39% under the minor beneficiary
  rule — same page.
- **DO NOT** recommend distributing income to beneficiaries. Slide 6 says it
  is a trustee decision that has to be made and recorded in time. That is all.
- Ring-fencing of rental losses applies to trusts too. Not needed on a slide.

## Caption
Hook: `If your rental is owned by a family trust and makes more than $10,000 a year, the profit the trust keeps is taxed at 39%.`

Then: since 1 April 2024, income that most family trusts retain is taxed at
39%, up from 33%. There is one concession. A trust earning $10,000 or less in
the year still pays 33%. Go over that and the higher rate applies. For a rental
with a small mortgage, a profit above $10,000 is ordinary, and many of these
trusts were set up long before the rate changed, on arrangements nobody has
reviewed since. The rate applies to income the trust does not distribute. What
happens to the profit is a decision for the trustees, and it has to be made and
recorded in time, not reconstructed afterwards. The useful step is to know the
rental's result before 31 March.
