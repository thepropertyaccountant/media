# 63 — The flat in Brisbane goes in your New Zealand return

**Audience:** residential rental owners with property overseas. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Keep it a few weeks clear of post 68.
**Source guide:** none on the site yet.
**Why it works:** Auckland has a great many people with a unit in Australia or
a flat in the UK who file there and assume that is the end of it.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/tax-by-rental-property-type/renting-out-an-overseas-property
  Last known: "As a New Zealand tax resident you'll pay tax on income from New
  Zealand and overseas. This includes tax on the rental income from your
  overseas rental property." "Even though your property is overseas, you'll
  still need to follow the New Zealand tax rules for your rental income." "You
  cannot put foreign currency amounts into your New Zealand tax returns."
- VERIFY against Interpretation Statement IS 20/06: "do not assume that
  deductions you are permitted for foreign tax purposes will be allowed under
  New Zealand's tax rules to the same extent or in the same way"; and that you
  "may be entitled to claim foreign tax credits in New Zealand for tax paid
  overseas" —
  https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/interpretation-statements/is-20-06.pdf
- VERIFY the credit limit, IR3 guide: the credit "is the lesser of the actual
  amount of tax paid on the overseas income or the amount of tax you would pay
  in New Zealand on the foreign income."
- VERIFY slide 6 before building it: that a foreign-currency mortgage can
  bring in the financial arrangements rules (and NRWT or AIL), and that a
  transitional resident's overseas rental income may be exempt for a period.
  Last known: both are flagged on the page and in IS 20/06; the transitional
  period was given as four years. **Name them, do not explain them.** If either
  cannot be confirmed, drop it from the slide.
- **DO NOT STATE** how to convert currency (which rates, which dates).
- The city in the hook is an example. Any overseas property is covered.

## Caption
Hook: `If you are a New Zealand tax resident with a rental property overseas, the rent goes in your New Zealand return.`

Then: New Zealand taxes its residents on income from anywhere, and that
includes rent from a property in another country. Filing a return over there
does not replace filing here. The income is worked out under New Zealand's
rules, so the deductions the other country allowed are not the test, and
everything is converted to New Zealand dollars. Tax paid overseas on the same
income may be credited against the New Zealand tax on it, up to the amount of
that New Zealand tax. Two things make it harder than it sounds: a mortgage in a
foreign currency has rules of its own, and people in their first few years as
New Zealand residents may be treated differently. If the overseas property has
never appeared in your New Zealand return, that is worth raising.
