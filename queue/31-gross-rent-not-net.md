# 31 — Your rent is what the tenant paid, not what landed in your account

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Strongest March to June, when returns are being
put together from bank statements.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** owners with a property manager routinely return the deposits.
The total may come out the same, but what the manager paid on the way through
gets lost, in both directions.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY: gross rental income is the rent **before** management fees. Last
  known wording: gross rental income includes "rental payments from tenants,
  for example $400 per week (this is the rental payment before you've deducted
  any management fees)" —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rules-for-working-out-rental-income-and-expenses/actual-cost-method
- VERIFY: the agent's fees are then deducted separately. IR264: "If you use an
  agent to collect the rent and/or maintain the property, the cost of the
  agent's fees can be deducted" —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
- VERIFY: the IR3R rental schedule has separate boxes for "Total rents" and
  "Agent's collection fees" — current IR3R form on ird.govt.nz.
- Slides 4 and 5 are practical observation, not a tax rule. Keep them worded as
  what goes wrong, not as what Inland Revenue says.
- Slide 3 is an illustration. Label it as an example and check the arithmetic:
  36,400 − 3,350 − 1,900 = 31,150.

## Caption
Hook: `Your rental income is the rent the tenant paid, not the amount the property manager sent on to you.`

Then: a manager collects the rent, takes the fee, pays the plumber and the
insurance excess, and deposits what is left. The return asks for the rent
before any of that, with the fees and the other costs claimed separately. Work
from the deposits instead and the total can look right while the detail is
wrong: a capital item the manager paid for ends up deducted in full without
anyone deciding that, or a real repair never reaches the return at all. The fix
is to work from the manager's annual statement, which lists the rent, the fees
and each payment on its own line. It is a better record than the bank feed and
it already exists.
