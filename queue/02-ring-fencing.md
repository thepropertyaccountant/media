# 02 — Ring-fencing: why a rental loss will not cut your salary tax

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Suggested:** 2026-11-23
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why now:** follows the interest-deductibility conversation; landlords assume
full interest deductions mean a refund against their salary. They do not.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY: residential property deduction (ring-fencing) rules still in force and
  apply from the **2019-20 income year** — IR264 "Rental income", ring-fencing
  section — https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property
- VERIFY: excess deductions carry forward and are used against **future
  residential rental income, or taxable income on sale** — same source.
- VERIFY: losses **cannot** offset salary, wages or business income — same source.
- VERIFY: the exclusions still listed (main home, a holiday home used privately,
  farmland, land used mainly as business premises) — same source.
- No dollar figures are required for this post. If the ledger on slide 5 uses
  amounts, they are illustrative, not tax figures — label them as an example.

## Caption
Hook: `Your rental ran at a loss and your salary tax will not move a cent.`

Then: the residential property deduction rules — ring-fencing — limit rental
deductions to rental income. A loss is not wasted, but it does not reduce tax
on your salary, wages or business income. It carries forward, to be used
against future rent from that property or against taxable income when you sell.
Full interest deductibility improved the paper position for a lot of landlords
without improving this year's cash at all, and that is the distinction worth
getting right before you budget around a refund. Forecast the rental on its own
footing.
