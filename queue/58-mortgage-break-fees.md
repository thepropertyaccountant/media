# 58 — Breaking a fixed rate on the rental loan? The fee is deductible.

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** when interest rates are falling and owners are breaking
fixed terms to refix, or any time.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** break fees can run to thousands and are routinely left out,
because the bank shows them as a fee and not as interest.

## Verify before building
**This post rests on binding public rulings, not on the rental guide. IR264
and the rental expenses page say nothing about break fees. Read the rulings.**
- VERIFY BR Pub 12/01 (break fee to exit a fixed-rate loan early) and BR Pub
  12/03 (break fee paid on sale of the rental property) —
  https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/rulings/public/pu12001-12003.pdf
  Last known: the break fee is taken into the base price adjustment under the
  financial arrangements rules, the resulting amount "will be interest", and a
  deduction is available.
- VERIFY the rulings are still in force. The status page showed "Indefinite",
  applying from the 2012/13 income year —
  https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/rulings/public/br-pub-1201-1203-deductibility-of-break-fees-paid-by-a-landlord-to-exit-early-from-or-vary-the-inter
- VERIFY the conditions and reflect them on slide 4. Last known: the rulings do
  not apply where the loan is not used solely for deriving rental income, where
  it is connected with other financial arrangements with the lender, or where
  the taxpayer uses an IFRS method. The slide carries the first; the caption
  should say conditions apply.
- BR Pub 12/02 (a fee to vary the interest rate without ending the loan) is in
  the same document. Its conclusion was not extracted. **Do not describe a
  rate variation fee unless 12/02 is read.** Slide 3 covers exit and sale only.
- VERIFY that current interest deductibility (100% from 1 April 2025) means an
  amount treated as interest is deductible in full —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rental-expense-deductions/property-interest-rules
- **DO NOT STATE** anything about cash-back or cash incentives from the new
  lender. Not checked.

## Caption
Hook: `If you paid a break fee to get out of a fixed rate on your rental loan, that fee is deductible.`

Then: Inland Revenue's public rulings treat a break fee paid by a landlord as
interest, and interest on a residential rental loan is deductible. The rulings
cover a fee paid to exit a fixed-rate loan early, and a fee paid because the
property was sold. Conditions apply, the main one being that the loan was used
solely for the rental. A loan that also funded private spending is outside the
rulings. The reason this deduction goes missing is mundane. The bank's
statement shows the break fee as a fee, so it is not in the interest total for
the year, and the interest total is the only number most people pass on. If you
broke a fixed term this year, find the bank's letter and add it to the list.
