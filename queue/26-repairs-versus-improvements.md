# 26 — Fixing it is deductible. Making it better is not.

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Summer is when the work gets done, so November to
February is a good window.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** the general repairs line, which the posted initial-repairs
carousel (9 Nov 2026) did not cover. That post was about work needed at
purchase; this one is about work on a rental you have held for years. Leave a
few weeks between this and post 52 (leaky buildings).

## Verify before building
- VERIFY Inland Revenue's wording of both sides, and the examples used on slide 5 —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rental-expense-deductions
  Last known: repairs "restore a property to its previous state" — examples
  replacing a broken window, repairing a hole in the Gib board, repainting the
  house. Capital improvements "add to the property and enhance it beyond its
  original state" — examples adding an extra room, installing heat pumps where
  none were present, installing double glazing.
- VERIFY the whole-asset point on slide 4 against Interpretation Statement
  IS 12/03: work that results in the "reconstruction, replacement or renewal of
  the asset, or substantially the whole of the asset" is capital —
  https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/interpretation-statements/is-1203.pdf
- VERIFY the garage roof example on slide 6 (IS 12/03 Example 11: a garage roof
  replaced with a comparable product was revenue). **Do not turn this into a
  statement about replacing the roof of the house** — no whole-house roof
  example was found on an Inland Revenue source.
- **DO NOT STATE** that a full kitchen or bathroom replacement is capital as a
  flat rule. No Inland Revenue example covers it. The slides stay with the
  published examples.
- The practice website's guide calls replacing "the whole roof with a better
  material" capital. That is consistent with the above, but cite IRD, not the site.

## Caption
Hook: `Fixing a rental is deductible. Making it better is not, and the invoice rarely tells you which one you did.`

Then: a repair puts the property back the way it was. A broken window, a hole
in the wall lining, a repaint. An improvement adds something or lifts the
property beyond what it was: an extra room, a heat pump where there was none,
double glazing. The first is deductible in the year you spend it and the second
is capital. The harder question is replacement. Renewing a part of something is
generally a repair; renewing substantially the whole of it is capital, even in
the same materials. Like for like helps and upgrading hurts. All of which is
decided by how the work is scoped and described before it starts, so the time
to think about it is before the quote, not when the invoice arrives.
