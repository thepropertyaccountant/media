# 12 — The withholding rate on your invoices is a choice

**Audience:** contractors and sole traders. **Suggested:** 2026-12-07
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html
**Why now:** a rate set on a new contract in December runs for the rest of the
income year, and the end-of-year surprise is still four months away.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY: a contractor receiving schedular payments can choose their own rate on
  the **IR330C**, and the minimum for a NZ resident is **10%** —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/withholding-taxes/schedular-payments/getting-schedular-payments/work-out-and-declare-my-tax-rate-for-schedular-payments
- VERIFY: the minimum is **15%** for a non-resident on a temporary work or entry
  visa — same page.
- VERIFY: the **no-notification rate of 45%** where sections 1 and 3 of the
  IR330C are not completed, and that it is **20%** for a non-resident contractor
  company — IR330C form, and
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/withholding-taxes/schedular-payments/about-schedular-payments-for-contractors
- VERIFY every standard rate used on slide 4 against the **current** IR330C
  (the version checked was January 2024, so look for a newer one). Last known:
  labour-only building work 20%, labour hire arrangement 20%, company directors'
  fees 33%, commercial cleaning 20%, forestry 15%, freelance contributions 25%.
- VERIFY: a rate below 10% needs a **tailored tax rate** application, and that a
  resident paid by a labour hire business under a labour hire arrangement cannot
  use a certificate of exemption but may apply for a 0% tailored rate.
- VERIFY: a tailored rate must be **reapplied for every tax year**.
- **DO NOT STATE** that schedular withholding counts as a provisional tax
  payment. That could not be confirmed on the schedular payment pages. If the
  post needs the provisional tax link, confirm it on the provisional tax pages
  first — otherwise keep slide 6 to the plain point that too low a rate leaves a
  bill at the end of the year.

## Caption
Hook: `The tax coming off your contracting invoices is a number you chose, or one that got chosen for you.`

Then: if your work gets schedular payments, the payer deducts tax before paying
you, and the rate comes off an IR330C you fill in — one for each payer, handed
to them rather than to Inland Revenue. Leave it blank and the no-notification
rate applies, which takes a very large bite. Fill it in and you can take the
standard rate for your activity or set your own, with 10% as the floor for a NZ
resident; anything lower needs a tailored rate you apply for and renew each
year. The rate is worth actual thought, because setting it too low does not save
you anything — it just moves the bill to the end of the year, when it arrives
with everything else. Quote only rates verified at build time.
