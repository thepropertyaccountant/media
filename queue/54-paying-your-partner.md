# 54 — Paying your partner for the admin needs sign-off first

**Audience:** self-employed real estate salespeople on commission. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. January to March is useful, while there is still
time to get approval before the return is filed.
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html (rules only, not framing).
**Why it works:** a very common arrangement — the partner who does the
database, the letterbox drops and the open-home signs — with a rule almost no
sole trader knows exists.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY against QB 19/01, "What are the requirements for claiming tax
  deductions for payments to family members?" —
  https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/questions-we-ve-been-asked/2019/qb-19-01.pdf
  Last known: "if the family member is a spouse or partner, the Commissioner's
  prior approval for a deduction is required ... unless the business is run
  though a company." "You have up until the time you file your tax return to
  get approval." "a deduction claimed without prior approval is denied", and
  the spouse "will still need to treat the payment as income."
- VERIFY what the application needs: the nature of the business, the services
  provided, the hours and weeks worked, what others would be paid, and the
  amount and method of payment — QB 19/01.
- VERIFY the two general conditions: "You must be able to show that the family
  member provided services to your business." "The amount paid must not be
  excessive for the services the family member provides." And: "You cannot
  split the income you earn between your family members for income tax
  purposes."
- VERIFY QB 19/01 is still current (issued March 2019). If it has been
  replaced, build from the replacement.
- VERIFY slide 7 — that prior approval is required only for a spouse or
  partner, and other family members need only meet the two general conditions.
  If not confirmed in those terms, cut slide 7.
- **DO NOT** go into PAYE or employer obligations. Paying a wage has them, the
  caption can say so in one clause, and the detail is another post.
- IR830A lists "wages you've paid (for example to personal assistants or
  salespeople)" as claimable. Useful background, not a substitute for QB 19/01.

## Caption
Hook: `If your partner does your admin and you pay them for it, you need Inland Revenue's approval before you can claim the deduction.`

Then: this applies to sole traders, which is how most salespeople operate. A
payment to your spouse or partner for work in your business is only deductible
if the Commissioner has approved it, and the approval has to be in place by the
time you file the return. Claim it without approval and the deduction is
denied, while your partner still returns the payment as income. That is the
worst of both. The application is not onerous: what they do, the hours, what
you would pay someone else for the same work, and how you pay them. The work
has to be real and the rate cannot be excessive, because the rule exists to
stop income being split to lower the tax. Paying a wage also brings employer
obligations of its own.
