# 30 — The suit is not deductible. Neither is the haircut.

**Audience:** self-employed real estate salespeople on commission. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time.
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html (rules only, not framing).
**Why it works:** something the reader will not enjoy hearing, said by Inland
Revenue in a fact sheet written for their own industry. Presentation is part of
the job, which is exactly why agents assume it is claimable.

## Caption
Hook: `The suit is not deductible. Neither is the haircut, the skincare or the sunglasses, even if your agency requires all of them.`

Then: Inland Revenue's fact sheet for the real estate industry is blunt about
this. Plain clothing worn at work is not deductible, even if your agency tells
you to wear it. Hairdressing, cosmetics, hair and skin products and sunglasses
are not deductible either, even where you are required to be well groomed. What
does qualify is distinctive work clothing, such as a uniform with a logo on it.
Looking the part is plainly part of the job, which is why the claim feels
reasonable, but the rule turns on what the thing is, not on why you bought it.
The practical point is that these are easy items to find in an expense list,
and leaving them in weakens the claims that are sound.
