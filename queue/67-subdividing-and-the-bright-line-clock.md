# 67 — Subdividing does not restart your bright-line clock

**Audience:** residential property owners with a subdividable section.
**Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Keep it a few weeks clear of post 09 (the
intention test) and the other bright-line posts.
**Source guide:** guides/bright-line-test.html
**Why it works:** Auckland's zoning has made the back section a live question
for ordinary owners. The bright-line answer is reassuring. The ten-year rules
behind it are not, and nobody has heard of them.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the start date —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/buying-and-selling/when-you-need-to-pay/the-brightline-test/start-and-end-dates
  Last known: for land you subdivided, the bright-line start date is "The
  original date of registration for the undivided property". Also in the
  bright-line guide IR1229 —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir1200---ir1299/ir1229/bright-line-property-tax.pdf
- VERIFY slide 4, IR1229: "The main home exclusion can apply to subdivided
  land even if there is no house on the subdivided section," where the land
  was used as the main home for more than 50% of the bright-line period. The
  guide also gives a counter-example where the exclusion did not apply. **Read
  both examples** and keep slide 4 no stronger than "can apply".
- VERIFY slide 5 against IR361 "Tax and your property transactions", which
  lists separate rules taxing subdivision and development within **10 years**
  of acquisition, independent of bright-line —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir300---ir399/ir361/ir361.pdf
  **This is the important slide and the least researched.** Confirm the rules
  exist as described, and name them only in plain words. Do not state their
  conditions or exclusions; they are detailed and were not read.
- VERIFY: bright-line period **2 years** for sales on or after 1 July 2024.
- **DO NOT STATE** that a section sale after two years is tax free. The whole
  point of slide 5 is that it may not be.

## Caption
Hook: `Subdividing your property does not restart the bright-line clock on the new section.`

Then: the bright-line start date for a section you have subdivided is the date
the original, undivided property was registered to you. If you have owned the
place for ten years, the new section is not a new purchase for bright-line
purposes, and where the land was part of your main home the main home exclusion
can apply to it as well. That is the reassuring half. The other half is that
bright-line is not the only test. Separate rules can tax the profit from
subdividing or developing land within ten years of buying it, and they do not
care what the bright-line clock says. Whether they apply depends on what was
done and how much, which is why the time for advice is before the surveyor is
engaged, not after the section has sold.
