# 53 — Sold inside two years at a loss? The loss is locked away.

**Audience:** residential rental owners and investors. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Suits a flat or falling market.
**Source guide:** guides/bright-line-test.html
**Why it works:** sellers assume the tax system is symmetrical — a taxable
gain, so a usable loss. It is not, and Inland Revenue's instruction not to put
the loss in the return surprises people.

## Caption
Hook: `Sell a property inside the bright-line period at a loss and the loss does not come off your other income.`

Then: a bright-line gain is taxable. A bright-line loss is not the mirror image
of that. It cannot be offset against salary, wages, rental income or business
income. It can only be used against profits from other taxable land sales, and
if there are none in the year you sell, it carries forward until there is one.
For someone who sells one property and never sells another inside the rules,
that may be never. Inland Revenue's guide also says not to include the loss in
your income tax return, which is why it tends to get forgotten. Keep the
workings: purchase, sale and costs. They are what you will need if a taxable
sale ever comes along to use it against.
