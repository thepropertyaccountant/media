# 39 — Not every body corporate levy is deductible

**Audience:** residential rental owners with apartments, units or townhouses.
**Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** Auckland has a lot of unit-title rentals, and owners claim
whatever the body corporate invoices. The split is Inland Revenue's own.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY all three statements on the rental expenses page —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rental-expense-deductions
  "General levies like those used to pay for annual maintenance or
  administration (such as painting or insurance) will normally be deductible."
  "Some levies may be raised to pay for capital improvements (for instance,
  those raised for a capital improvement fund). These are not deductible."
  "An amount levied may relate to both repairs and capital improvements. To
  claim a deduction in these instances you must be able to reasonably determine
  the portion of the levy that relates to repairs."
- Inland Revenue does not use the phrase "special levy". Do not use it on a
  slide as if it were a tax category — a special levy can fund either kind of
  work. Describe levies by what they pay for.
- **DO NOT STATE** how a levy for leaky-building remediation is treated. That
  turns on whether the work is repair or improvement (see post 52) and cannot
  be put flatly.
- Slide 6 is practical advice, not a stated rule.

## Caption
Hook: `Not every body corporate levy on your rental is deductible. It depends on what the levy is paying for.`

Then: the general levy that covers annual maintenance, administration and
insurance is normally deductible. A levy raised to pay for capital improvements
is not, even though it comes from the same body corporate on the same
letterhead. Where one levy covers both repairs and improvements, you can claim
the repair portion only if you can reasonably work out what that portion is.
The levy notice rarely tells you. The body corporate's budget and meeting
minutes usually do, and they are worth asking for before the return is
prepared, not after a question is raised about it.
