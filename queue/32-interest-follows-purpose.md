# 32 — Secured on the rental does not mean deductible

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Leave some weeks between this and post 07
(interest back to 100%), which it complements.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** with interest fully deductible again, the top-up on the
rental mortgage is the live mistake. The rule is short and Inland Revenue's
wording is quotable.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the core wording. IR264 lists as not deductible: "interest on money
  you borrow for some purpose other than financing the rental property, even if
  you use the rental property to secure the loan" —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
  The rental expenses page says the same in near-identical words —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rental-expense-deductions
- VERIFY: "From 1 April 2025 you can claim the interest charged on any money
  you borrow to finance your rental property. However, you cannot claim all the
  interest as an expense if you borrowed the money for another purpose as well
  as buying your rental property." — IR264.
- VERIFY: IR264 on top-ups — where a home loan has been topped up "for another
  purpose not related to your residential rental property, the interest is not
  deductible".
- **DO NOT STATE** the reverse case as an Inland Revenue example (a loan
  secured over your own home, used to buy the rental, being deductible). It
  follows from "any money you borrow to finance your rental property" but no
  explicit example was found. The post stays on the top-up.
- Avoid the word "tracing" on slides. IRD's current pages use it only for
  pre-27 March 2021 mixed loans.
- Slide 5 is an illustration. Label it as an example.

## Caption
Hook: `A loan secured over your rental is not automatically a rental loan, and the interest on it is not automatically deductible.`

Then: interest on a residential rental is fully deductible again, which makes
this the mistake of the moment. Deductibility follows what the borrowed money
was used for. Money borrowed to finance the rental qualifies. Money borrowed
for something else does not, and Inland Revenue says so directly: even if you
use the rental property to secure the loan. The usual version is a top-up on
the rental mortgage that paid for a car or a renovation at home. That slice of
interest is private. The simple protection is structural: keep any top-up in
its own loan account, so the bank's statements split the interest for you
instead of leaving you to apportion a mixed balance every year.
