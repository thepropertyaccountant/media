# 36 — Year one on commission is not tax free. It is deferred.

**Audience:** self-employed real estate salespeople on commission, especially
those in their first or second year. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** November to March. The voluntary payment that earns the
discount has to be made before the end of the income year (31 March).
**Source guide:** guides/provisional-tax.html (rules only, not framing).
**Why it works:** the classic second-year squeeze, plus a discount almost
nobody claims. Keep it clear of held post 06 (15 January instalment) and post
22 (smoothing), which are for established earners.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY every statement on —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/provisional-tax/paying-tax-in-your-first-year-in-business
  Last known: "you will not need to pay provisional tax during your first year
  of business"; "However, your first year in business is not tax free."; "Any
  income tax from your first year in business is usually due by 7 February the
  following year."; "If you have an agent it will be due by 7 April."; and that
  you may be paying it "at the same time as paying provisional tax for your
  second year in business."
- VERIFY the early payment discount **rate and the year it belongs to**. It
  changes every year. Last known: **4.25% for the 2027 income year** (the year
  to 31 March 2027); it was 6.30% for 2026 and 6.67% for 2025. **Do not use
  6.7%** — that figure is out of date. Name the income year on the slide.
- VERIFY the discount conditions on the same page: self-employed (or a partner,
  or a look-through company owner) with most income from the business; a
  voluntary payment made before the end of the income year; applied for by the
  return filing date; no provisional tax obligation in that year or the
  previous four.
- VERIFY: provisional tax applies where the end-of-year tax bill from the last
  return was **more than $5,000** — https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/provisional-tax
- Slide 5 touches the schedular payment question the task prompt marks as
  unresolved. It is worded conditionally ("if tax is withheld") and asserts no
  rate. Keep it that way, or confirm IR830 (see post 33) before sharpening it.
  If in doubt, cut slide 5 and run seven slides.
- **DO NOT** call the discount interest, and do not describe how it is
  calculated beyond the rate. The page's example applies the rate to the lesser
  of the payments and 105% of the year's tax; leave that detail out.

## Caption
Hook: `Your first year on commission is not tax free. The tax is just not asked for yet.`

Then: in your first year in business there is no provisional tax, so nothing
falls due while you are getting established. Inland Revenue's own page is
careful to add that the year is not tax free. The bill for it is usually due
on 7 February the following year, or 7 April with a tax agent, and if it comes
in over $5,000 the provisional instalments for year two start around the same
time. That is the squeeze people describe as being taxed twice. It is two
years' tax meeting in the same few months. There is one piece of good news that
almost nobody uses: make a voluntary payment before the end of your first year
and you can apply for an early payment discount on it. The rate is set each
year, so name the year it applies to. Either way, the February number is
knowable well before February.
