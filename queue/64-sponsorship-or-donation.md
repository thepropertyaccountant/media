# 64 — Sponsoring the school fair is advertising, if you do it right

**Audience:** self-employed real estate salespeople on commission. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Suits February to March (school and club
sponsorship season) and October to November (galas and fairs).
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html (rules only, not framing).
**Why it works:** agents sponsor everything in their patch. Whether it is a
business deduction, a donation credit or neither depends on paperwork they
rarely have.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the sponsorship rules against fact sheet IS 26/10 FS 1 (April 2026) —
  https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/fact-sheets/2026/is-26-10-fs-1.pdf
  Last known: "To be deductible, sponsorship expenditure must have a sufficient
  connection to your business's income-earning process." "This requirement will
  be met if the sponsorship is intended to advertise or promote your business."
  Where there is "a private benefit that is more than incidental, it will be
  only partly deductible." Where entertainment is received, "the deduction is
  limited to 50% of what would otherwise be allowed." Its example: a $3,000
  tennis club sponsorship with logo display, "The expenditure is fully
  deductible."
  The full statement IS 26/10 was not read. Read it if anything on a slide
  goes beyond the fact sheet.
- VERIFY the donation tax credit —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-individuals/individual-tax-credits/donation-tax-credits/about-donation-tax-credits
  Last known: "Individuals can claim 1/3rd of the value of eligible donations
  over $5, up to their taxable income." The donation must be to "an approved
  donee organisation", with a receipt. Not claimable: "donations providing a
  direct benefit to you or your family."
- **Check whether the donation tax credit has changed.** A 2026 tax policy
  paper on changing it was seen in search results and not read. If the credit
  has been altered or is under announced change, rewrite slide 5 to match or
  cut it.
- Inland Revenue says "1/3rd", not "33.33%". Use "one third".
- Slide 7 is practical advice, not a stated requirement.

## Caption
Hook: `Sponsoring the school fair or the local club is a deductible advertising cost, provided it is actually set up as advertising.`

Then: sponsorship is deductible where it is intended to advertise or promote
your business: your name on the banner, in the programme, on the team's shirts.
Inland Revenue's own example is a $3,000 club sponsorship with the logo
displayed, fully deductible. Two things change the answer. If part of what you
get back is tickets or hospitality, that part is treated as entertainment and
limited to half. And if there is no promotion at all, it is not a business
expense. It may be a donation, which for an individual can earn a tax credit of
one third, with a receipt from an approved organisation. A payment that buys
you something is not a donation, so it cannot be both. A short written
agreement that says what you get for the money settles which one it is.
