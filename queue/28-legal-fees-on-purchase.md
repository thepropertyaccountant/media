# 28 — The legal bill for buying the rental may be deductible

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Useful January to March for anyone who settled a
purchase during the income year.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html — **note the conflict
below before building.**
**Why it works:** almost everything on a settlement statement is capital, so
this one gets thrown out with the rest. A dollar threshold and a catch.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the rule and its exact wording. Last known, IR264 (March 2026 edition):
  "You can also claim legal fees as part of buying a rental property provided
  your total legal fees for the income year are $10,000 or less" —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
  The rental expenses page words it more loosely ("legal fees involved in
  buying a rental property, if the expense is $10,000 or less") —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rental-expense-deductions
- VERIFY the catch on slide 3: the test is **total legal fees for the income
  year**, not the conveyancing bill on its own. Use IR264's wording.
- **DO NOT STATE** anything about legal fees on **selling**. IR264 lists
  "real estate agent or legal fees charged as part of selling the property" as
  non-deductible, with an exception for people in the business of providing
  residential rental accommodation. That exception was not pinned down. Keep
  the post to the purchase.
- VERIFY slide 4 before building: that above $10,000 the special rule does not
  apply and the ordinary capital test decides each bill. This follows from the
  wording but was not found stated in words. If it cannot be confirmed, cut
  slide 4 and run the post at six slides.
- **Conflict with the practice website:** the rental deductions guide lists
  "Legal and other costs of buying or selling" under what you cannot claim.
  IR264 says otherwise for purchase legal fees within the $10,000 limit. Build
  from IR264, and mention the conflict in the run report so the guide can be
  corrected.
- Slide 5 figures are an illustration. Label it as an example.

## Caption
Hook: `The solicitor's bill for buying your rental may be deductible, even though nearly everything else on the settlement statement is not.`

Then: the purchase price, the deposit and most of the costs around them are
capital. Legal fees have their own rule. Where your total legal fees for the
income year are $10,000 or less, the fees for buying a rental property can be
claimed. The catch is in the word total: it is every legal bill for the year
added together, not the conveyancing invoice on its own. For a single ordinary
purchase the bill is usually well inside the limit, and it is usually missing
from the return, because it was filed away with the rest of the settlement
papers. If you settled on a rental this income year, find the invoice.
