# 42 — Renting to family at mates' rates caps your deductions

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. December and January suit it, when family
arrangements get made.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** a common Auckland arrangement — the adult child in the
rental at half rent — with a consequence the parents never expect.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY both sentences, IR264 (March 2026 edition) —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
  "If you rent your property out for less than its true rental value, for
  example, to a relative or friend at mates' rates, and you still make a profit
  from it, the profit is taxable as part of your income."
  "But if you make a loss in this situation, because the expenses of the
  property are more than the reduced rental income, you generally will not be
  able to deduct expenses more than the amount of income you received."
- Keep the word "generally" on slide 3. Inland Revenue uses it.
- **DO NOT STATE** a percentage of market rent as a threshold. The 80% figure
  in IR264 belongs to the holiday home (mixed-use asset) rules only.
- **DO NOT STATE** whether the disallowed excess carries forward. Not found.
  The slides say the deduction is capped and stop there.
- Slide 6 is practical advice, not a stated requirement.
- Slide 5 is an illustration. Label it and check the arithmetic: 350 × 52 = 18,200.

## Caption
Hook: `Rent your property to family at mates' rates and your deductions are generally capped at the rent you actually received.`

Then: if the reduced rent still leaves a profit, nothing unusual happens and
the profit is taxed like any other rent. The difference shows when the property
runs at a loss. Where the costs exceed the reduced rent, you generally cannot
deduct more than the income you received. The gap between market rent and what
you chose to charge is a private decision, not a cost of earning rent, so the
loss it creates is not a tax loss. That does not make the arrangement a bad
one. Plenty of families decide it is worth it. It is better decided knowing the
market rent and what the discount does to the return, which a written rental
appraisal settles for very little.
