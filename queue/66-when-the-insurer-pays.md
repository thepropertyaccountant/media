# 66 — The insurer paid for the repair. You cannot claim it too.

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. After a storm or flood season it lands hardest.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** owners either claim the whole repair as if the insurer never
paid, or declare the payout as income and claim nothing. Both are wrong.

## Caption
Hook: `If the insurer paid for a repair to your rental, you cannot also claim the repair.`

Then: Inland Revenue's guide deals with it neatly. An insurance payout to
repair a damaged asset is not included as income, and the repair cost the
insurance covered is not claimed. The two cancel. What you can deduct is any
part of the repair the payout did not cover, which is usually your excess. If
the payout comes to more than the repair cost, the surplus reduces the asset's
book value and, past a point, becomes income. And if the asset was destroyed
and not repaired, the payout is treated as if you had sold it for that amount.
The common mistakes run in both directions: claiming the full repair as though
no payout existed, or declaring the payout and claiming nothing. Keep the
insurer's settlement letter with the repair invoice.
