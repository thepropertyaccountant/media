# 23 — A room on Airbnb: the first $65 a night is not income

**Audience:** residential rental owners / homeowners letting a room. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** late November to February, when spare rooms and sleepouts go
on short-stay platforms for the summer. Works any time.
**Source guide:** none on the site yet.
**Why it works:** a number, a hard limit and a trade-off most hosts have never
heard of. Distinct from post 11 (the bach), which is a separate property.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the nightly standard-cost rates and the income year they belong to.
  Last known, for the **2025-26 income year: $65 a night if you own the home,
  $59 if you rent it** —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rules-for-working-out-rental-income-and-expenses/short-stay-standard-cost-for-renting-out-your-home-or-its-rooms
  The rate is CPI-adjusted each year and published around June for the year just
  ended (DET 19/02 adjustments on taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz). **Check for a newer
  rate and name the income year on the slide.**
- VERIFY: the **100 nights or less** limit, and that each room counts as a night
  (two rooms for one night is two nights) — same page.
- VERIFY the conditions: it is your own main home; stays are no more than
  **4 consecutive weeks**; the accommodation is not part of a GST taxable
  activity; no boarders in the same home; nobody claims actual costs or another
  standard cost for the home in the same year — same page.
- VERIFY: rent up to the standard cost is **exempt income** with no return to
  file; only the excess over the rate is taxable; **no expenses can be claimed**
  on top — same page.
- VERIFY Inland Revenue's own example before using it on slide 5: 60 nights at
  $85 is $5,100, less 60 × $65 = $3,900, leaving $1,200 taxable. If the rate
  has moved, the example on the page will have moved too — use the page's.

## Caption
Hook: `Rent out a room in your own home on Airbnb and the first $65 a night is not income.`

Then: Inland Revenue publishes a standard cost per night for short-stay
accommodation in your own home. Charge up to that figure and the income is
exempt, with no tax and no return for it. Charge more and only the excess is
taxable. The conditions are tight: it has to be your own main home, 100 nights
or fewer in the year with each room counted separately, and no stay longer than
four weeks. The trade is that you claim no actual expenses on top, because the
nightly figure is the deduction. For a spare room over summer it is usually the
simplest answer there is. For a separate property, or anything over 100 nights,
it is not available and the ordinary rules apply. Name the income year the rate
belongs to, because it changes every year.
