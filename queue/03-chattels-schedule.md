# 03 — The chattels schedule nobody set up at purchase

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Suggested:** 2026-11-30
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why now:** follows naturally from post 02 — the deduction landlords are
most often entitled to and least often claiming.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY: the building itself cannot be depreciated — IR264, and
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rental-expense-deductions
  (lists "depreciation on the rental's land or buildings" as non-deductible).
- VERIFY each diminishing value rate used, from IR264 Table 3, assets acquired
  on or after 1 April 2011. Last known: carpets **25%**, curtains/blinds **25%**,
  stoves and ovens **25%**, dishwashers and washing machines **30%**,
  heat pumps (through wall or window type) **20%**,
  electric or gas hot water cylinders **13%**, loose furniture **20%**.
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
- VERIFY: low-value asset full deduction threshold, last known **$1,000** from
  17 March 2021 — IR264, low value asset section.
- VERIFY: a retrospective chattels schedule is still permitted before claiming
  it is — check the depreciation guidance rather than asserting it flatly.

## Caption
Hook: `Your rental building cannot be depreciated. Nearly everything inside it can.`

Then: carpets, curtains, blinds, appliances, heat pumps, hot water cylinders —
each depreciates over its own life at Inland Revenue's rates, and anything under
$1,000 comes off in full the year you buy it. This is the most commonly
under-claimed deduction a landlord has, and the reason is almost always the
same: no chattels valuation was done when the property settled, so there was
never a schedule to depreciate from. Setting one up after the fact is possible
and usually worth the exercise. Quote the rates verified at build time.
