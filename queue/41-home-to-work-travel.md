# 41 — The drive to the office is not a business trip

**Audience:** self-employed real estate salespeople on commission. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Keep it a few weeks clear of posts 08 (kilometre
rates) and 44 (logbook).
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html (rules only, not framing).
**Why it works:** agents live in their cars and assume every kilometre is
business. The fact sheet for their industry says otherwise, in one sentence.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the wording, IR830A "Business expenses in the real estate industry"
  (August 2026; look for a newer edition): "You generally cannot claim the cost
  of trips between home and work, even if you live a long way from your usual
  workplace" —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir800---ir899/ir830a/ir830a.pdf
  Keep the word "generally" on slide 2.
- VERIFY slide 4 against the general vehicle expenses guidance: that travel
  between places of work in the course of the business is business travel —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses
  If the page does not support the examples as worded, reduce slide 4 to "trips
  made in the course of the work" and drop the specific legs.
- **DO NOT STATE** that working from a home office turns the trip from home
  into business travel. No Inland Revenue statement was found for the
  self-employed, and it is exactly the claim a reader will want to make.
- **DO NOT STATE** a rule for the first or last appointment of the day.
  Slide 5's example deliberately starts and ends at the office.
- Slide 6 is practical observation, not a stated rule.

## Caption
Hook: `The drive from home to the office is not a business trip, however far out you live.`

Then: Inland Revenue's fact sheet for the real estate industry says you
generally cannot claim the cost of trips between home and work, even if you
live a long way from your usual workplace. The business kilometres are the ones
driven in the course of the work: from the office to an appraisal, from a
listing to an open home, from one viewing to the next. For someone who drives
all day that still adds up to a substantial claim. But the commute at each end
belongs in the logbook as private, and it lowers the business percentage. Put
it in as business and the percentage looks better right up until someone reads
the logbook.

General-information line, then hashtags.
