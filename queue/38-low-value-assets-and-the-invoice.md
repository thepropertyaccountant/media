# 38 — Under $1,000 comes off in full. Unless you bought six at once.

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Leave some weeks between this and post 03
(chattels schedule), which mentions the $1,000 threshold in passing.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** everyone has heard of the $1,000 write-off. Almost nobody has
heard the condition attached to it, which bites exactly when a landlord fits
out a rental in one order.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the threshold and its history, IR264 (March 2026 edition, low-value
  assets): "up to 16 March 2020 - up to $500"; "17 March 2020 to 16 March 2021
  - up to $5,000"; "17 March 2021 onwards - up to $1,000" —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
- VERIFY the catch, which is the post. IR260 "Depreciation - a guide for
  businesses": the immediate deduction applies provided the assets "are not
  purchased from the same supplier at the same time as other assets to which
  the same depreciation rate applies" —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir260/ir260.pdf
  **CONFIRMED 14 November 2026**, IR260 (September 2026 edition) page 21,
  "Low value assets (including loose tools)". The guide says low value assets
  are deductible in the year acquired "provided: they are not purchased from
  the same supplier at the same time as other assets to which the same
  depreciation rate applies". So the condition is real and is IRD's own.
- **But note exactly what the guide does NOT say, and keep slide 4 inside it.**
  IR260 states the proviso and stops. It does not say the assets are added
  together and tested against the $1,000, it gives no example, and IR264 does
  not carry the condition at all. So slide 4 must put it as IRD does — bought
  from one supplier at one time, same depreciation rate, and the immediate
  deduction is not available — and must NOT claim the six are tested as a
  $5,400 group. That framing is an inference, not the guide's words.
- The "six heaters" example may stay only as an illustration of same supplier,
  same time, same rate. If that cannot be made to read cleanly on the slide,
  drop the example and quote the proviso instead.
- Pooling is a different mechanism (IR260: pooled assets are "treated as 1
  asset", maximum $5,000 per asset). Do not blend it into this post.
- VERIFY whether IR264 itself carries the same condition. If it does, cite
  IR264; it is the rental guide.
- Slide 6's $950 heat pump is an illustration, not a price.

## Caption
Hook: `Anything under $1,000 for the rental comes off in full the year you buy it. Unless it came with five more just like it.`

Then: the low-value asset rule lets you deduct the whole cost of an asset of
$1,000 or less in the year of purchase, instead of depreciating it. It is the
most used rule in a chattels schedule and the least read. The condition is that
the asset was not bought from the same supplier at the same time as other
assets with the same depreciation rate. Bought together, they are looked at
together. So one heater is a write-off, and a matching set for every room on a
single invoice is a depreciation schedule. Nothing is lost, since the cost
still comes off over the life of the assets, but the timing is different and
the return needs to show it the right way.

General-information line, then hashtags.
