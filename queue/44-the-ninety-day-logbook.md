# 44 — Ninety days of logbook buys three years of claims

**Audience:** self-employed real estate salespeople on commission. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** late January to March — a logbook started then finishes
inside the income year. Keep it a few weeks clear of posts 08 and 41.
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html (rules only, not framing).
**Why it works:** post 08 compares the two vehicle methods. This one is only
the logbook: what goes in it, how long it lasts, and what the claim falls to
without one.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the logbook rules —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses/use-a-logbook
  Last known: "keeping a logbook for at least 90 consecutive days"; "Your
  calculation can be used for up to 3 years if the proportion of business use
  doesn't change by more than 20%."
- VERIFY what must be recorded, same page: the start date and odometer reading;
  the date, distance and reason for each business journey; the end date of the
  90-day period and the odometer reading.
- VERIFY the cap without a logbook: "The claim will be limited to 25% of the
  vehicle running costs as a business expense." —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses
  IR320 "Smart business" puts it as "you may claim up to 25%" and adds that
  "you could be asked to verify the percentage claimed". Use "up to" or
  "capped at", not a flat entitlement to 25%.
- Slide 5's examples of what changes business use are illustrations, not IRD's.
- Slide 7 is practical advice, not a stated rule.

## Caption
Hook: `Ninety days of logbook supports three years of vehicle claims. Not keeping one caps the claim at a quarter.`

Then: the logbook is a test period, not a lifetime habit. Keep it for at least
90 consecutive days: the odometer at the start, then the date, distance and
reason for each business trip, then the odometer at the end. The business-use
percentage it produces can be used for up to three years, provided your
business use does not shift by more than 20%. Without a logbook, the claim is
limited to 25% of the vehicle's running costs, and you can still be asked to
show that even that much is right. For someone who drives between appraisals
and open homes all week, the real figure is usually far higher than a quarter.
Ninety days of notes is what it costs to claim it.
