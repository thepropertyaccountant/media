# 57 — Income protection premiums may be deductible. Life cover is not.

**Audience:** self-employed real estate salespeople on commission. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. February and March suit it, before the year closes.
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html (rules only, not framing).
**Why it works:** commission earners have no sick leave and often carry income
protection. Most either claim every premium on the insurance statement or none.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the test —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-individuals/types-of-individual-expenses/non-business-expenses
  Last known, listed as claimable: "The cost of income protection insurance if
  the insurance payout would be taxable." "Ask your insurance provider if your
  income protection insurance is deductible (can be claimed as an expense)."
  "This is also called 'loss of earnings' insurance."
- VERIFY the payout side —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-individuals/types-of-individual-income/insurance-payouts
  Last known: a payout replacing lost income is "generally taxable however, you
  should check the exact terms of your insurance policy". "Amounts paid out
  under personal sickness insurance policies are generally excluded from being
  taxable income." "However, if the amount paid out is calculated in reference
  to loss of earnings this may be classed as taxable income." That page is
  titled around COVID-19 and dated 2021 — look for a newer general page.
- VERIFY life insurance: IR320 "Smart business" lists "private expenses, such
  as life insurance" as non-deductible —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir300---ir399/ir320/ir320.pdf
- **DO NOT USE** the terms "agreed value" or "indemnity". No Inland Revenue
  source for the self-employed was found using them. Describe the policy by
  whether the payout is calculated by reference to lost earnings.
- **DO NOT STATE** anything about health, trauma or mortgage protection cover
  by name. Slide 5 keeps to the three rows that are sourced.
- Keep "may be" in the hook. The answer depends on the policy.

## Caption
Hook: `Income protection premiums may be deductible. Life insurance premiums are not, and the two often arrive on the same statement.`

Then: on commission there is no sick leave, so a lot of salespeople carry
income protection. Whether the premium is deductible depends on the payout. If
a claim would pay out an amount that replaces lost income and is taxable, the
premium is deductible. If the payout would not be taxable, the premium is
private. Life insurance is always on the private side. Policies differ in how
the payout is calculated, which is why Inland Revenue's own advice is to ask
your insurance provider whether yours is deductible. It is one email. Get the
answer in writing, keep it with the year's records, and split the premiums on
the statement accordingly instead of claiming all of it or none.
