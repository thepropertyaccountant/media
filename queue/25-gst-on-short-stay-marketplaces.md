# 25 — Airbnb collects 15% GST on your listing. You may get 8.5% back.

**Audience:** residential property owners with short-stay listings. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** November to February, the short-stay season. Works any time.
**Source guide:** guides/gst-registration.html (for the threshold only).
**Why it works:** the rule changed on 1 April 2024 and many hosts still do not
know why their payout statements changed, or that taking the credit while GST
registered is an error. Run it apart from post 29, which covers the sale.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY: from **1 April 2024** online marketplaces collect GST at **15%** on
  listed services including short-stay accommodation —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/sharing-economy/sellers-of-listed-services/gst-for-listed-services
- VERIFY: where the host is **not GST registered**, the marketplace pays
  **6.5%** to Inland Revenue and passes **8.5%** to the host as a **flat-rate
  credit** — same page, and fact sheet AD277,
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ad277/goods-and-services-gst-on-listed-services.pdf
- VERIFY: a **GST-registered** host must tell the marketplace, returns the
  bookings as a zero-rated supply, and is not entitled to the flat-rate credit.
  Credits received in error are repaid by a debit adjustment, and penalties may
  apply if they keep being received — same sources.
- VERIFY: short-stay accommodation is a taxable activity and counts toward the
  **$60,000** registration threshold —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/gst-and-renting-out-residential-property/gst-and-your-short-stay-rental-income
- VERIFY before mentioning income tax on the credit: a host can choose to treat
  flat-rate credits as assessable income or as excluded income —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/sharing-economy/sellers-of-listed-services/flat-rate-credits-and-income-tax
  Leave this out of the slides unless there is room; it is a caption point.
- These rules cover bookings through a marketplace. Direct bookings follow the
  ordinary GST rules. Do not blur the two.

## Caption
Hook: `Since 1 April 2024, Airbnb and the other platforms collect 15% GST on your listing whether you are registered or not.`

Then: the marketplace now does the collecting. If you are not GST registered,
it sends 6.5% to Inland Revenue and passes the other 8.5% to you as a flat-rate
credit, which stands in for the GST on costs you have no return to claim in. If
you are registered, the platform needs to know: the credit is not yours, your
bookings go into your own return as zero-rated, and you claim your costs the
ordinary way. Taking the credit while registered is an error that has to be
repaid, and repeated it can bring penalties. None of this removes the
threshold. Short-stay accommodation is a taxable activity, so the income counts
toward the $60,000 test, and a good summer can cross it.
