# 35 — Inherited the house? The bright-line test does not apply.

**Audience:** residential rental owners and their families. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time.
**Source guide:** guides/bright-line-test.html
**Why it works:** good news with two exceptions attached, both of which catch
families who settle an estate between siblings.

## Caption
Hook: `If you inherited a property, the bright-line test does not apply when it passes to you, or when you later sell it.`

Then: the transfer from the estate is not taxed under the bright-line test, and
neither is the sale by the person who inherited, however soon it follows. Two
things sit alongside that. If you bought out a brother's or sister's share to
end up with the whole property, that share was purchased, not inherited, and it
is inside the bright-line period from the day you acquired it. And Inland
Revenue's position is that you inherit the previous owner's intentions for the
property along with the title, so the other land rules can still apply to a
sale. Before listing, it is worth having the will, the transfer and any
buy-out agreement in front of you, because they decide which share is which.
