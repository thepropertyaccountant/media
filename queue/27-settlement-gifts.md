# 27 — The champagne is half. The tea towels are whole.

**Audience:** self-employed real estate salespeople on commission. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** December, when settlement gifts and client thank-yous peak.
Works any time. Keep it a few weeks clear of post 14 (entertainment).
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html (rules only, not framing).
**Why it works:** Inland Revenue's entertainment guide uses a real estate agent
as its worked example for gifts. The post can quote the regulator talking
directly to the audience.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the rule: gifts of food and drink that give a private benefit to the
  recipient and a business benefit to the giver are **50% deductible** — IR268
  "Entertainment expenses" (April 2024 edition was the one read; **look for a
  newer one**) —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir268/ir268-2024.pdf
- VERIFY the worked example on that page before using it on slide 5. Last
  known: real estate agent Bob gives a bottle of champagne to the owner when a
  sale is arranged — 50%. He sends the purchaser a basket with wine, cheese,
  tea towels and soaps — the tea towels and soap are fully deductible "because
  these items are not food and drink", the wine and cheese 50%.
- VERIFY: "If the cost cannot be identified separately, then an appropriate
  amount should be apportioned" — same page.
- VERIFY the real estate fact sheet IR830A (August 2026): "only 50% of gifts of
  food and drink can be claimed" and "The gift must have a link to earning your
  income" —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir800---ir899/ir830a/ir830a.pdf
- **DO NOT STATE** that flowers, vouchers or any other named gift are 100%
  deductible. Inland Revenue's example covers tea towels and soap only. The
  slides say "not food, not drink" and stop there.
- **DO NOT STATE** a GST rule for the 50% portion. Not confirmed.

## Caption
Hook: `The bottle of champagne you leave the vendor at settlement is half deductible. The tea towels in the same basket are fully deductible.`

Then: that is Inland Revenue's own example, and it is written about a real
estate agent. Gifts of food and drink are treated as entertainment, because the
person receiving them enjoys them privately, so only 50% of the cost is
deductible. Anything in the gift that is not food or drink is not caught by
that rule. Where a hamper comes at one price, the food and drink part has to be
split out on a reasonable basis. Two practical points follow. Keep the receipt
and a note of which sale the gift related to, because a gift only counts at all
if it links to earning your commission. And code the bottle and the rest
separately as you go, because nobody remembers in April what was in the basket.
