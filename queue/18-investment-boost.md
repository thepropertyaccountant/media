# 18 — Investment Boost: 20% off the top of a new asset

**Audience:** contractors and sole traders. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Slight lean to February or March, when people buy
equipment before balance date.
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html

## Verify before building
This is a newer measure than the rest of the queue, so verify it as a whole,
not just its figures.
- VERIFY: from **22 May 2025**, businesses can claim **20% of the cost of new
  assets** as an expense, then depreciate the remaining **80%** as usual —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/new-assets---investment-boost
  (note the three hyphens in that slug) and its sub-pages
  `/what--you-can-claim-with-investment-boost` and `/investment-boost-benefits`
- VERIFY: it is **accelerated depreciation**, not an extra deduction — IRD's own
  words are that it "does not change the total value of deductions" over the
  asset's life. **The post must say this.** Selling it as free money would be
  wrong and would read as spin.
- VERIFY: the asset must be **new, or new to New Zealand**, and **first
  available for use** on or after 22 May 2025; "available for use" means
  physically and legally able to be used.
- VERIFY the exclusions: **second-hand assets sourced from New Zealand**,
  **residential rental buildings**, and most fixed-life intangibles such as
  patents.
- VERIFY: "There is **no limit** to the value of new investments you can claim
  Investment Boost for", and that there is no minimum.
- VERIFY: no end date has been announced. **Do not call it temporary or say it
  is ending** unless an end date is confirmed on the day.
- **DO NOT STATE**, without checking first, that it applies to vehicles, tools
  or equipment specifically, or that sole traders qualify. IRD's pages say "all
  businesses" and list general criteria but name none of these. Read the
  "Investment Boost examples" sub-page, which was not checked, before building —
  if it confirms them, the post can be specific; if not, keep the post to
  "new business assets" and drop slide 4.
- VERIFY how it is claimed: included in the return, no notification to IRD, and
  for IR10 filers boxes 28, 52 and 60. Keep the box numbers out of the slides —
  too fiddly for the format — but they can go in the caption if confirmed.

## Caption
Hook: `Buy a new business asset and 20% of its cost comes off in the first year. It is not a bonus, and that part matters.`

Then: Investment Boost lets a business deduct 20% of the cost of a qualifying
new asset up front, then depreciate the remaining 80% the ordinary way. Inland
Revenue is direct about what it is: accelerated depreciation. The total
deduction over the asset's life does not change. What changes is when you get
it, which is a real cash flow gain and worth planning around, but it is not a
tax cut and anyone selling it to you as one is overstating it. The asset has to
be new, or new to New Zealand, and first available for use on or after 22 May
2025 — so second-hand gear bought locally is out, as are residential rental
buildings. There is no cap on the value. If you have equipment to buy anyway,
when it becomes available for use is the detail that decides which year the
deduction falls in.
