# 51 — Throwing out the old carpet can be a deduction

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Run it a few weeks after post 03 (chattels
schedule) and before post 13 (depreciation recovery) — 03 sells the schedule,
this shows the write-off, 13 shows the bill at the other end.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** the replacement gets recorded because there is an invoice.
The old asset's remaining value quietly stays on the schedule forever.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the rule, IR264 (March 2026 edition, selling or disposing of assets) —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
  "If you sell or dispose of a rental asset that is not a building for a
  different amount to its adjusted tax value, you need to account for the
  difference - either a loss or a gain."
  "If you sell an asset for less than its adjusted tax value, you can claim a
  deduction for the difference between the sale price and the adjusted tax
  value."
- IR264 says "sell or dispose". The words "scrapped" and "thrown out" do not
  appear. Slide 1's "throwing out" is fair as a kind of disposal, but confirm
  the passage reads as covering disposal for nothing before building. IR260
  adds: "You may be able to write off the residual tax value of any depreciable
  asset that you no longer use to derive gross income." —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir260/ir260.pdf
- VERIFY slide 4: "You're allowed a deduction for the cost to dismantle,
  demolish and remove an asset." — IR264.
- VERIFY slide 5: "Losses on the sale or disposal of buildings are not
  deductible" — IR264.
- **Pooled assets are different.** IR260: "If the asset is discarded or lost,
  the adjusted tax value of the pool is not reduced." If slide 5 has room, it
  can say so; otherwise note it in the caption. Do not imply the write-off
  applies to assets depreciated in a pool.
- Slide 3 is an illustration. Label it and check the arithmetic:
  6,000 − 4,300 = 1,700.

## Caption
Hook: `When you throw out the rental's old carpet, whatever is left of its book value can come off as a deduction.`

Then: a chattel on your depreciation schedule has a book value that drops each
year. If you dispose of it for less than that figure, including for nothing,
you can claim the difference. The cost of pulling it up and taking it away is
deductible as well. Two limits apply. It does not work for the building, where
losses on disposal are not deductible. And it only works for assets that were
on the schedule in the first place, because something nobody recorded has no
book value to write off. Assets depreciated together in a pool follow a
different rule. In practice the new carpet gets recorded because an invoice
arrives, and the old one sits on the schedule for years. A note of what went
in the skip, and when, fixes it.
