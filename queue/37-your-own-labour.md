# 37 — You can claim the paint. You cannot claim the weekend.

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** December to February, when owners do the work themselves
over the holidays. Works any time.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** short, concrete and slightly unwelcome. Every hands-on
landlord has wondered about it.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the wording, IR264 (March 2026 edition): "If you do the repairs
  yourself, you cannot claim your time as an expense, only the materials you
  purchase." —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
- VERIFY: the rental expenses page lists "your time when you do repairs and
  maintenance work" under what cannot be deducted, and "travel expenses for
  travelling to inspect your property or to do repairs" under what can —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rental-expense-deductions
- **DO NOT STATE** anything about time spent *managing* the property (finding
  tenants, inspections, paperwork). No Inland Revenue statement was found
  either way. The post is about repairs and maintenance only.
- **DO NOT STATE** that hired equipment or tools are deductible. Not checked.
  Slide 4 names only materials and travel.
- Slide 6 assumes the work is a repair. Say so — paying someone does not make
  an improvement deductible (see post 26).
- Slide 5 figures are an illustration. Label it as an example.

## Caption
Hook: `Repaint the rental yourself and you can claim the paint. You cannot claim the weekend.`

Then: Inland Revenue's guide says it plainly. If you do the repairs yourself,
you cannot claim your time as an expense, only the materials you purchase. A
deduction needs a cost, and you did not pay anyone for the labour. What does
count is the materials and the travel to the property to do the work. The other
side of this is worth a moment before the summer jobs start. Pay a painter and
the whole invoice is deductible, labour included, provided the job is a repair
and not an improvement. Doing it yourself saves the cash and gives up the
deduction on the labour. Sometimes that is still the right call. It is better
made knowingly, with the receipts kept either way.
