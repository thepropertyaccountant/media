# 16 — What underpaying actually costs

**Audience:** contractors and sole traders. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** shortly after a provisional tax instalment date (28 August,
15 January, 7 May) when people have just paid, or just not paid.
**Source guide:** guides/provisional-tax.html

- VERIFY both use of money interest rates and the date they took effect. Last

  does **not** apply to income tax, provisional tax or GST —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/managing-my-tax/penalties-and-interest/penalties-and-debt/late-payment-penalties

  period IRD may allow one before charging penalties.
- VERIFY: on the standard option with residual income tax **under $60,000**,
  interest runs only from the day after the **end-of-year tax due date**, not
  from the missed instalment —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/provisional-tax/interest-on-provisional-tax

## Caption
Hook: `Inland Revenue charges you about four times the interest rate it pays you. Worth knowing which side of that you are on.`

Then: two separate things happen when you pay late. Use of money interest runs
daily on what you owe, at a rate set in legislation and updated as market rates
move. On top of that, late payment penalties apply from the day after the due
date, with more added a week later. They are not alternatives; you can get
both. Two things soften it. Under- and overpayments of $100 or less are left
alone. And on the standard option with residual income tax under $60,000, the
interest clock does not start at a missed instalment — it starts after your
end-of-year date, which is a genuine piece of breathing room that a lot of
contractors do not know they have. The penalties are not so forgiving: those run
from the due date regardless. Quote only the rates verified at build time, and
say the date they took effect.
