# 46 — The bond is not income. Until you keep some of it.

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** December to February, when tenancies end and bonds are
claimed. Works any time.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** every landlord handles a bond and almost none know which
part of a bond claim goes in the return. Short and specific.

## Verify before building

  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf

  "Amounts out of a tenancy bond you receive from MBIE for payment of rent
  arrears, should be included as income."
  "Amounts out of a tenancy bond you receive because of damage to the property

- **Known conflict — resolve it before building slide 4.** The actual cost
  method web page (last updated June 2024) lists "payments from the Tenancy
  Board for damages or rent arrears" as gross rental income —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/residential-rental-income-and-paying-tax-on-it/rules-for-working-out-rental-income-and-expenses/actual-cost-method

## Caption
Hook: `A tenant's bond is not rental income. It can become income when the tenancy ends, depending on what you keep it for.`

Then: bond money you collect and lodge with MBIE is not income. It is not
yours. If the tenancy ends with rent owing and part of the bond is released to
you for the arrears, that part is income, because it is simply the rent you
were owed, arriving late. One related point catches people at this time of
year: rent a tenant pays in advance is taxable in the year you receive it, not
the year it covers. So when a bond claim comes back, the useful habit is to
note what each dollar was for at the time. The Tenancy Services paperwork says
it once. Nobody remembers it the following June.

General-information line, then hashtags.
