# 19 — The marketing you pay for is deductible. If it stays paid by you.

**Audience:** self-employed real estate salespeople on commission. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Keep it a few weeks clear of post 61, which lists
marketing among other claims from the same fact sheet.
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html (rules only, not framing).
**Why it works:** marketing is the biggest cost most salespeople carry
themselves, and the one where who finally paid is least clear at year end.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY against IR830A "Business expenses in the real estate industry"
  (August 2026; look for a newer edition) —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir800---ir899/ir830a/ir830a.pdf
  Last known: "advertising & marketing" is listed under other expenses you can
  claim, and the general tests are "you must have spent the money yourself and
  were not reimbursed"; "it must be directly related to earning your business
  income"; "you must have a record to prove it."
- Slides 4 and 5 apply the "not reimbursed" test to vendor-paid and
  agency-paid marketing. That is the fact sheet's own condition applied to
  common cases, not an Inland Revenue example. Keep the slides worded as who
  bore the cost. **DO NOT STATE** how a vendor's contribution is treated in the
  agent's income; it was not found.
- VERIFY slide 6: a GST-registered person can claim GST on expenses of the
  taxable activity, and needs to hold taxable supply information for them —
  IR375 GST guide,
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir300---ir399/ir375/ir375.pdf
  IRD's term is now "taxable supply information", not "tax invoice".
- VERIFY slide 7: IR320 "Smart business": "In general a website is a capital
  asset and the costs must be capitalised and depreciated." —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir300---ir399/ir320/ir320.pdf
  If that sentence is not found, cut slide 7.
- **DO NOT STATE** that any particular item (photography, staging, drone
  footage, signboards, social media advertising) is deductible by name. The
  fact sheet says "advertising & marketing" and no more.
- Sponsorship is post 64. Gifts are post 27. Keep both out.

## Caption
Hook: `Marketing you pay for out of your own commission is deductible. Marketing you were reimbursed for is not.`

Then: Inland Revenue's fact sheet for the real estate industry lists
advertising and marketing as claimable, on one condition that matters more
here than anywhere: you must have spent the money yourself and not been
reimbursed. At year end that is harder to answer than it sounds. A campaign the
vendor paid for may have gone through your card. The agency may have covered
part of one and recovered part of another from your commission. The deduction
is only ever the share that finally came out of your pocket. The fix is to
record who paid, in the end, against each campaign as it finishes. If you are
GST registered, the GST on your share is claimable too, with the supplier's
invoice on file. A website is generally a capital asset and is treated
differently.
