## Caption
Hook: `Healthy Homes compliance work is not automatically a deduction.`

Then: the ordinary repairs-and-maintenance line still applies. Work that fixes
or maintains something already there is generally deductible. Work that renews
substantially the whole asset, or installs something that was never there, is
capital — and on a residential building capital spend does not depreciate, so
it waits until you sell. Fitting a heat pump into a house that had no heating is
the example that catches most landlords, because it feels like maintenance and
is treated as an addition. Use IRD's own examples, verified at build time. Then
the timing point: a deductible repair falls in the year you incur it, which is
what makes 31 March worth planning around.
