# 29 — GST registered for the Airbnb? The house is in the GST net too.

**Audience:** residential property owners with short-stay listings. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Keep it at least two weeks clear of post 25, which
covers the marketplace rules.
**Source guide:** guides/gst-registration.html (for the threshold only).
**Why it works:** the single most expensive short-stay mistake. Owners register
to claim GST on the purchase and discover the other end of the deal when they
sell, or when they simply switch to a long-term tenant.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY both limbs, in Inland Revenue's words —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/property/renting-out-residential-property/gst-and-renting-out-residential-property/gst-and-selling-or-changing-the-use-of-a-rental-property
  Last known: if you have "sold your residential rental property, you need to
  return GST output tax on the sale price"; if you have "stopped short-term
  renting, you need to return GST output tax on the market value of the
  property."
- VERIFY against fact sheet AD277 (August 2025), which says the GST rules
  "will generally apply to the sale" and that GST is also payable "if you stop
  using your property ... for this purpose" —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ad277/goods-and-services-gst-on-listed-services.pdf
  It also sets out when the sale can be non-taxable: only if no GST deduction
  was previously claimed, the property was not bought or used mainly for making
  taxable supplies, and it was not bought zero-rated. **Read this before
  building** — it limits how flatly slides 3 and 4 can be put.
- VERIFY slide 6: for a host who is not registered, the GST rules "will
  generally not apply to the sale", unless they intend to make supplies of more
  than **$60,000** in the next 12 months — AD277.
- VERIFY the GST fraction of **3/23** of a GST-inclusive amount before using it
  on slide 5 — IR375 GST guide.
- **DO NOT STATE** what happens on deregistration as a separate rule. The only
  source found was a 2020 interpretation statement (IS 20/04).
- Slide 5 figures are an illustration. Label it as an example and check the
  arithmetic: 920,000 × 3 / 23 = 120,000.

## Caption
Hook: `If you registered for GST to run a short-stay property, the property itself is inside the GST net, not just the bookings.`

Then: registering lets you claim the GST on the purchase and the fit-out, which
is why people do it. The other end of that deal arrives later. Sell the
property and GST output tax is due on the sale price. Stop short-stay letting
and put a long-term tenant in instead, and GST is due on the market value of
the property at that point, with no sale and no cash to pay it from. Because
property usually rises in value, the GST going out can be well above the GST
that came in. For a host who is not registered, none of this generally applies.
The decision to register is made once, at the start, and it is worth making
with the exit in the numbers.
