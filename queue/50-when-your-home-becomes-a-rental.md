# 50 — Moving out and renting the house? Value the chattels that week.

**Audience:** homeowners about to become landlords. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** December to February, when people move. Works any time.
Leave some weeks between this and posts 03 and 51.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** the accidental landlord is a large part of the audience, and
the one thing that has to be done on the day is the one thing nobody does.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the rule, IR264 (March 2026 edition, depreciation section) —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
  "If 1 of your personal assets becomes part of your rental activity, use its
  market value at the time of the change to use as the opening book value for
  depreciation."
  "If you start to rent out the home that you were living in, you need to use
  the market value of your chattels at the time you started renting the
  property to calculate depreciation."
- VERIFY the reverse on slide 6: "If you keep an asset, but stop using it for
  rental purposes, you'll have to make an adjustment as if you'd sold it for
  its market value at the start of the next tax year. For example, you take an
  asset from your rental property for your own personal use or you move into
  the property." — IR264.
- VERIFY: "From the 2011-12 income year depreciation on buildings is 0%." — IR264.
- **DO NOT STATE** anything about the mortgage interest becoming deductible
  from the changeover date. It is very likely the reader's next question, and
  no explicit Inland Revenue statement on a former home was found. If the run
  finds one, it may add a slide; otherwise leave it out.
- **DO NOT STATE** anything about the main home exclusion on a later sale.
  That is post 05.
- Slide 5 is an illustration. Label it as an example.
- Slide 7 is practical advice, not a stated requirement.

## Caption
Hook: `If you are moving out and renting your house, the chattels need valuing the week you leave.`

Then: when a home becomes a rental, the carpets, curtains, appliances and heat
pump become rental assets, and they can be depreciated. The figure you
depreciate from is not what you paid for them. It is their market value at the
time the property started being rented. The years of private use are gone, and
only what is left counts. That makes the changeover date the one moment the
values can be set properly: a list, some photos, the age of each item and a
sensible second-hand figure. It works in reverse too. Move back in later and
the chattels are treated as sold at market value, which can bring back some of
the depreciation claimed. The building itself is not depreciable either way.
