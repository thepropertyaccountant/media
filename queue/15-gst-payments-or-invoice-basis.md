# 15 — Paying GST on money you have not been paid

**Audience:** contractors and sole traders. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. No seasonal hook.
**Source guide:** guides/gst-registration.html

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the three bases and, precisely, what triggers GST under each —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/registering-for-gst/which-gst-accounting-basis-and-filing-frequency-should-i-use
  Payments basis: amounts **you have been paid**. Invoice basis: amounts you
  have **notified customers to pay by invoice, even if you have not been paid
  yet**. Confirm this wording; it is the post.
- VERIFY: the payments basis is available where total sales are **$2 million or
  less** in the last 12 months, or likely to be in any 12-month period. Note the
  page lists the invoice and hybrid bases as open to "anyone" and does **not**
  state in words that businesses above $2m must use the invoice basis — so do
  not say they must.
- VERIFY: filing frequency thresholds — six-monthly under **$500,000**,
  two-monthly under **$24 million**, monthly compulsory over $24 million.
- VERIFY: the basis is chosen **at registration**.
- **DO NOT STATE** how to change basis after registration, or that it is easy.
  That could not be confirmed on this page. Slide 7 should say to ask, not
  assert a process. If a source is found, state it properly instead.
- Keep the hybrid basis out of the post unless there is room. IRD notes it is
  "not commonly used by small businesses because of negative cashflow
  consequences", which is a good line if a slide needs it.

## Caption
Hook: `You can owe GST on an invoice your client has not paid you for yet. It depends on a box someone ticked when you registered.`

Then: on the invoice basis, a sale goes into your GST return when you issue the
invoice, whether or not the money has arrived. On the payments basis it goes in
when you are actually paid. For a contractor invoicing on 30-day terms across a
period end, that is the difference between funding the GST yourself and not.
The payments basis is open to businesses with sales of $2 million or less,
which is most sole traders and contractors, and the choice is made when you
register — so plenty of people are on a basis they never really chose. Worth
knowing which one you are on before the next return rather than after it.
