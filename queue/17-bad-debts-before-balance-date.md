# 17 — The invoice that never got paid

**Audience:** contractors and sole traders. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** February or early March — far enough before a 31 March
balance date that the reader can still act on it.
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html

## Verify before building
Source is **BR Pub 18/07**, "Income tax and goods and services tax — writing off
debts as bad", issued 29 August 2018, applying from 1 September 2018 for an
indefinite period. It replaced BR Pub 05/01, which is withdrawn — **do not cite
05/01**.
https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/rulings/public/pub00300-august2018.pdf
- VERIFY FIRST: that BR Pub 18/07 has not itself been amended or withdrawn since
  2018. Check IRD's rulings list before building. If it has been replaced, read
  the replacement and rebuild from that.
- VERIFY: the write-off "must take place before the end of the income year or
  GST taxable period" and **cannot be backdated** (para 36).
- VERIFY: a decision alone is not enough — "there must be something written down
  in the business's account books stating that the debt is written off"
  (para 31), and that for a small unincorporated business a dated **note in the
  bookkeeping records** suffices (para 33), or a dated written note on the
  invoice placed in a bad debts file (para 40). This nuance matters for a sole
  trader audience; do not imply they need a formal ledger.
- VERIFY: the bad debt test — "no reasonable likelihood that the debt will be
  paid", payment "more than merely doubtful", and that age alone does not make a
  debt bad (paras 8, 9).
- VERIFY: that a prudent commercial person would usually take recovery steps
  first (para 16).
- VERIFY: the GST side for an invoice-basis taxpayer — a deduction from output
  tax of the tax fraction of the debt, claimable in the period of the write-off.
  Note s 26(1AB) limits relief for **payments-basis** taxpayers and was not read;
  either confirm it or keep the post to the invoice basis.
- VERIFY: recovery afterwards is income in the year received, with a matching
  GST consequence (paras 24, 25).
- **FRAMING CORRECTION — do not write the obvious version of this post.** Missing
  the write-off before balance date does **not** lose the deduction. It moves it
  to the year the write-off is made, provided the debt is still bad then. The
  real rule is no backdating. Slide 5 says this correctly; keep it that way.
- Say "your balance date", not "31 March", unless the post states it is assuming
  a standard balance date.

## Caption
Hook: `Deciding an invoice will never be paid is not the same as writing it off, and only one of them is deductible.`

Then: two things have to be true. The debt has to actually be bad — no
reasonable likelihood of payment, which is a higher bar than merely doubtful
and is not satisfied by age alone. And it has to be written off: something
written down in your records, dated. A decision in your head does not count. For
a sole trader without formal accounts, a dated note in the bookkeeping is
enough, so this is not a reason to build a ledger. The timing matters because a
write-off cannot be backdated — do it after your balance date and the deduction
belongs to next year instead, which is a cash flow problem rather than a
disaster. If you are on the invoice basis you have already paid GST on that
invoice, and writing the debt off is also when you claim that back. And if the
client surfaces with the money two years later, it comes back as income then.
