# 34 — Empty between tenants. Still deductible.

**Audience:** residential rental owners. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** December to February, the main tenancy changeover period.
Works any time.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** reassurance with an edge. The costs keep counting through a
vacancy, but only while the property is available, and owners rarely keep the
evidence that it was.

## Verify before building
- VERIFY all three sentences, IR264 (March 2026 edition, section on periods
  when the property is not rented) —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir264/ir264.pdf
  "You can claim a deduction for any expenses that you incur while your rental
  property is either rented out or is available to be rented out."
  "But if the property is not occupied by tenants or available for rent for
  part of the year, you cannot claim the full year's ongoing costs, such as
  rates, insurance and interest."
  "If a property is not occupied or available for letting for a short time,
  because of redecorating or other maintenance, you can still get a deduction
  for the ongoing costs for that period."
- VERIFY slide 5's last row against IS 12/03 Example 2 (repairs after the owner
  moved in were not deductible because the rental activity had ceased) —
  https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/interpretation-statements/is-1203.pdf
- **DO NOT STATE** a number of weeks or months that counts as "a short time".
  Inland Revenue gives none.
- Slide 6 is practical advice about evidence, not a stated requirement. Keep it
  worded that way.

## Caption
Hook: `A rental that sits empty between tenants is still a rental for tax, as long as it was genuinely available to rent.`

Then: you can claim the ongoing costs while the property is rented out or
available to be rented out. A short gap for redecorating or maintenance between
tenants does not break that. What does is a period where the property is
neither let nor available: held back for family, used by you, or simply not on
the market. For that part of the year the rates, insurance and interest do not
count. The rule is generous and the weak point is proof. If the property sat
empty for two months over summer, the listing and the letting agent's emails
are what show it was available the whole time. Keep them with the year's
records.
