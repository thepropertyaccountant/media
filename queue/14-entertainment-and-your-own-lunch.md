# 14 — Entertainment is half. Your own lunch is nothing.

**Audience:** contractors and sole traders. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** late November to December — Christmas functions and client
lunches make it land. Works any time, but that is its best window.
**Source guide:** guides/contractor-deductions.html

## Verify before building
- VERIFY: entertainment with a "significant private element" is **50%**
  deductible, and the 50% applies whether the private element is more or less
  than half —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/entertainment-expenses
  IRD's own 50% examples: corporate box at a sports or cultural event; business
  use of a holiday home; hiring a boat with food and drink; food and drink at
  social events such as parties; gifts of food and drink enjoyed privately.
- VERIFY: the **100%** cases — entertainment enjoyed **outside New Zealand**;
  food and drink at a conference or course lasting **at least 4 hours**;
  entertainment that promotes the business where the public has the same access;
  entertainment secondary to a trade display. Same page.
- VERIFY the central claim of the post, which is the surprising one: a
  self-employed person's **own meals are usually a private expense** and
  generally not deductible —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/meal-expenses
  Confirm the narrow exception: extra cost only, where you have no choice but to
  pay more because you work in a **remote location** or at **unusual hours**,
  and that "just working away is not enough".
- **DO NOT STATE** that a self-employed person's meals while travelling on
  business are 100% deductible. IRD's 100% travel-meal wording is framed around
  an **employee**, and the meal page's rule for the self-employed is the
  "usually private" one. If the post needs this, confirm it first on the Tax
  Technical fact sheet IS 21/06 FS 1 (Treatment of meal expenses).
- **DO NOT STATE** a GST adjustment rule for a 50% entertainment claim. It could
  not be confirmed. The meal page says only that GST follows income tax: if you
  cannot claim the expense you cannot claim the GST.
- Both IRD pages were last updated in 2021. Check for newer wording, and check
  the IR268 entertainment guide, before building.

## Caption
Hook: `Lunch with a client is half deductible. The sandwich you eat alone at the job is not deductible at all.`

Then: most contractors have this backwards. Your own meals are treated as a
private expense, because you would have eaten either way — the only give is
where you have no real choice but to spend more, working genuinely remote or at
unusual hours, and even then only the extra. Entertaining someone else is the
opposite: a client lunch, the Christmas do, drinks for the crew all come in at
half, on the reasoning that there is a real private element in them whatever
the split actually was. A few things sit at the full hundred — a conference
running four hours or more, entertainment that promotes the business to the
public, and, oddly, entertainment outside New Zealand. The practical advice is
to code them apart as you go, because separating a year of mixed receipts in
April costs more in time than the deduction returns.
