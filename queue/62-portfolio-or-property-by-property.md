# 62 — Two rentals, one at a loss. Can it offset the other?

**Audience:** residential rental owners with two or more properties. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time. Leave some weeks between this and post 02
(ring-fencing), which it builds on.
**Source guide:** guides/rental-property-deductions.html
**Why it works:** owners with a second property have made a one-way choice
without knowing there was one.

## Caption
Hook: `If you own two rentals and one runs at a loss, whether that loss reduces the tax on the other depends on a choice most owners never knowingly made.`

Then: ring-fencing keeps rental losses away from your salary. Inside the fence
there are two ways to do the sums. On the portfolio basis, which is the
default, your rentals are pooled, and excess deductions on one property can be
used against income from another. On the individual property basis each
property stands alone. The individual basis has to be chosen from the first
year the rules apply to a property, and once a property has been on the
portfolio basis it cannot be moved off it. So for most owners the question is
already settled, and the useful thing is to know which way. It matters most
when you are buying another property or thinking about selling one.
