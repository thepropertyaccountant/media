# 22 — This year's provisional tax is sized on last year's income

**Audience:** self-employed real estate salespeople on commission. **Status: BANK**
**Best deployed:** any time outside the fortnight before 15 January, which
belongs to held post 06. Strongest in April to July, when the new year's
instalments are being set, and after a strong year.
**Source guide:** guides/provisional-tax.html (rules only, not framing).
**Why it works:** the structural problem of commission income, and four
options where most people know of one. Distinct from post 06 (the January
date) and post 36 (the first year).

## Verify before building
- VERIFY the standard option —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/provisional-tax/provisional-tax-options/standard-option
  Last known: based on "your previous year's residual income tax (RIT) plus
  5%", or "your RIT from 2 years ago plus 10%" where last year's return is not
  yet filed. Inland Revenue says "plus 5%", not "105%".
- VERIFY the estimation option —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/provisional-tax/provisional-tax-options/estimation-option
  Last known: "You can estimate your provisional tax at any provisional tax
  instalment date"; "You will be charged interest if your provisional tax
  estimate is less than your residual income tax"; a penalty is possible if
  the estimate is too low.
- VERIFY AIM —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/provisional-tax/provisional-tax-options/accounting-income-method-aim
  Last known: "available to individuals and companies with a yearly turnover
  under $5 million"; needs AIM-capable accounting software; "you only pay
  provisional tax when your business earns a profit."
- VERIFY the ratio option —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/provisional-tax/provisional-tax-options/ratio-option
  Last known: "lets you match your provisional tax payments with your business
  cashflow"; conditions include being GST registered for the whole previous
  year, RIT over $5,000 and up to $150,000, GST filed monthly or two-monthly,
  and applying before the income year starts. **Confirm how the ratio is
  applied before using slide 6's wording** ("a percentage of your GST sales").
  If the page does not put it that way, use IRD's "match your provisional tax
  payments with your business cashflow" on the slide instead.
- VERIFY slide 7 —
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/provisional-tax/interest-on-provisional-tax
  Last known: on the standard option, "If your residual income tax (RIT) is
  less than $60,000, we'll charge or pay you interest from the day after the
  end-of-year tax due date."
- **DO NOT USE** the phrase "safe harbour". It is not on the IRD page.
- **DO NOT** recommend an option. The post lays out four and says to choose.
- Tax pooling is a fifth mechanism (registered intermediaries,
  https://www.ird.govt.nz/topics/intermediaries/tax-pooling). It is left out
  to keep the post to Inland Revenue's own options; do not add it without
  reading those pages.

## Caption
Hook: `Your provisional tax this year is based on what you earned last year, which is a poor fit for commission income.`

Then: the standard option takes last year's tax bill, adds 5% and splits it
across the instalments. After a strong year that means paying big-year
instalments out of thin-year commission and waiting for a refund. It is the
default, not the only choice. You can estimate, and pay on what you expect this
year to be, re-estimating as the year unfolds, with interest if you come in
low. The accounting income method works from your actual results through
approved software, so you pay when you are making a profit. The ratio option
ties the payments to your GST sales. Each has conditions and a cost in
bookkeeping. And on the standard option there is a cushion: where the year's
tax is under $60,000, interest runs from the end-of-year due date and not from
each instalment. The method is worth choosing on purpose.
